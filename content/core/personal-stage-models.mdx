# Personal stage models

A personal stage model is a classifier trained on your own nights: the Pod's heart rate, HRV, breathing, and movement are the inputs, and the stages your Apple Watch recorded on the same nights are the labels. It is scored with Cohen's kappa (κ) against the Watch on nights it never saw, beside two baselines: the Pod's [sleep-stage rules](/core/settings/sleep-stages/) and the bundled challenger model that ships in the iOS app.

This is a development preview. The Pod side is pinned to core development revision `09e10015` and the phone side to iOS development revision `3fe45f1a`, from the development branches; availability depends on your installed build.

## Explore models in the training workspace

For live training progress, historical runs, and side-by-side Pod / candidate /
baseline / Watch timelines, see the [ML training workspace](/developers/training/).
That separate research app saves candidates locally without activating them;
the core script documented below includes an upload step.

## What gets trained

Two models, taken from the open sleep_classifiers work on wrist-worn data:

- **Logistic regression**, the safer result with few nights.
- **Random forest**, 200 trees of depth 8, which needs more nights before its score settles.

Each is trained on one of two feature contracts:

| Contract | Inputs                                                                          | Why it exists                                                        |
| -------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **A**    | The four inputs the bundled challenger uses                                     | Parity with the challenger, so a personal fit can be compared fairly |
| **B**    | Pod native: contract A plus HRV, breathing, and 15-minute windows (the default) | Uses everything the Pod measures                                     |

Rows are five-minute epochs labeled by the Watch stage at the epoch midpoint. Rare stages are oversampled rather than weighted. Scoring is leave-one-night-out: each night is predicted by a model trained on the others, and κ is reported pooled and per night. The per-night values matter; with ten nights, one lucky pooled number is easy to get.

## Train on a Mac (recommended)

Training runs in a few seconds on Apple silicon and nothing runs on the phone. You need:

- Watch stages already on the Pod for the side you are training. Import a Health export with `scripts/import-watch-health.py` from the core repository, or from the browser under [Sleep → Biometrics](/core/biometrics/#explore-vitals-and-import-watch-data).
- Root SSH to the Pod on port 8822 (see [Open the Pod and get root](/core/root-access/)).
- [uv](https://docs.astral.sh/uv/), which fetches NumPy and scikit-learn for the run.

From a checkout of the core repository:

```bash
scripts/train-personal-models.py --pod POD_IP --side left
```

One run does all of this:

1. Takes a consistent snapshot of the Pod's `biometrics.db` over SSH and copies it to the Mac.
2. Builds the per-minute dataset of Pod vitals with Watch labels for that side.
3. Trains both models on the chosen contract with leave-one-night-out scoring.
4. Exports each model in the Pod's portable JSON form and checks it against scikit-learn on every training row.
5. Uploads the model with the higher pooled κ to the Pod with `PUT /api/sleep-stages/model`.

Useful flags:

| Flag                                | Effect                                                             |
| ----------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `--side left` / `--side right`      | Which sleeper to train for (default `left`)                        |
| `--contract A` / `--contract B`     | Feature contract (default `B`)                                     |
| `--kind logistic` / `--kind forest` | Upload a specific model instead of the higher-scoring one          |
| `--no-upload`                       | Train and export only                                              |
| `--snapshot PATH`                   | Reuse an existing `biometrics.db` snapshot instead of taking one   |
| `--work DIR`                        | Working directory (default `./personal-models`)                    |
| `--keep-snapshot`                   | Keep the database snapshot after the run; it is deleted by default |

**Footprint.** A run leaves the dataset and the exported models, about 0.8 MB, in the work directory. The database snapshot is about 75 MB and is deleted after the run unless you pass `--keep-snapshot`. The Pod keeps a model library per side and one selected model. The script’s legacy upload replaces the selection and preserves its active state (a first upload defaults to inactive). A depth 8 forest is about 2 MB.

## What the Pod does with it

The Pod stores the model and evaluates it with its own TypeScript, computing the same feature contracts from a night's vitals and movement. Routes under `/api`:

| Method | Path                          | Purpose                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| ------ | ----------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| GET    | `/sleep-stages/model`         | The side's selected model, upload time, and active flag                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| PUT    | `/sleep-stages/model`         | Replace the selected model, preserving its active state (inactive on first upload), or `null` to remove it. The Pod refuses a model that names an unknown feature contract or fails any of its own test vectors, so a converter mismatch is caught at upload |
| POST   | `/sleep-stages/model/preview` | Stage recent nights with the stored model (or one sent inline) and score it against the Watch beside the side's rules on the same nights                                                                                                                     |

```bash
curl --fail -X POST http://POD_IP:3000/api/sleep-stages/model/preview \
  -H 'content-type: application/json' -d '{"side":"left","nights":14}'
```

Preview scores are in-sample when the previewed nights were also in the training set, which they are after a normal run: the earlier depth 4 contract B forest that scored κ 0.20 leave-one-night-out reached κ 0.33 when the Pod previewed its own training nights. Treat the preview as a check that the Pod evaluates the model the same way the Mac did, not as independent validation; the held-out κ from training is the number to compare.

The selected model can stage the side's nights when active. The saved rules remain
available and are the fallback for nights the model cannot stage. Turning the model
off returns to the rules without removing the selection; stage reads identify the
source with `stagedBy`.

For a candidate that should not replace the selection immediately, use the library:

| Method | Path                            | Purpose                                                |
| ------ | ------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| GET    | `/sleep-stages/models`          | List a side's models and selected/active flags.        |
| POST   | `/sleep-stages/models`          | Add `{side, model}` without selecting; returns an ID.  |
| PUT    | `/sleep-stages/models/selected` | Select `{side, id, active}` after reviewing a preview. |
| PUT    | `/sleep-stages/model/active`    | Set `{side, active}`; `false` returns to the rules.    |

For agent-assisted library management and the full-forest curl upload, see
[MCP personal stage models](/core/mcp/#personal-stage-models). Inline MCP models
have a 256 KB limit; full forests go through the REST upload. Training with
`--no-upload` lets you review and add a candidate before selecting or activating it.

## On the iPhone

The app fetches the Pod's model on every load of a night and evaluates the same document the same way the Pod does.

- **Sleep → Night → Compare stage models** shows a **Pod model** lane beside the rules, the bundled challenger, and the Watch, with its score and the leave-one-night-out κ it was trained with.
- **Settings → On-device analysis → Sleep stages from** offers **Pod model** for the stages the app shows and syncs to Health.

On-device training with Create ML remains for people without a Mac. It is slow: a forest run on about ten nights takes around 20 minutes in the foreground, iOS suspends it the moment the phone locks, and it never runs on background refresh. Keep the phone unlocked and the app open, or train on a Mac instead.

## What the numbers mean

On the developer's own Pod, with eleven left-side nights, the contract B forest at depth 8 reached κ 0.25 held out against the Watch. The bundled challenger scored κ 0.12 on the same nights and the rules about 0.02. Contract A, with the challenger's four inputs, did not beat the challenger with either model.

Depth 8 came from a leave-one-night-out sweep on those nights with 200 trees: κ 0.18, 0.21, 0.22, 0.26, and 0.23 at depth 3, 4, 6, 8, and unlimited. Using 100 to 1000 trees made no difference at any depth, and logistic regression stayed flat at κ 0.10 to 0.11 across regularisation strengths from 0.01 to 100. Histogram gradient boosting at depth 3 reached κ 0.27, within the noise of the forest and needing its own export format, so it is a later candidate rather than a change.

Read these as agreement with the Watch, not accuracy. The Watch is itself an estimate, at roughly κ 0.6 against a sleep lab, so a personal model can at best learn to agree with it. The scores will move as nights accumulate; the forest in particular needs 15 to 20 nights before its κ stabilises. HRV may be defined differently on the Pod and the Watch; see the note on metric identity in [Sleep stages](/core/settings/sleep-stages/#preview-before-saving).

sleepypod is not a medical device. Stage estimates, from the rules or from a personal model, are for trends and curiosity, not diagnosis.
