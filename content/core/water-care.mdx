# Water care and maintenance liquid

For **Pod 5 and earlier**, sleepypod favors **dilute liquid benzalkonium chloride (BZK, also called BAK or BKC)** added through the reservoir for water treatment.[^water-care] Measure it; do not pour in an unmeasured splash. This guide describes the available community dosing evidence and its limits. It does not establish that liquid treatment outperforms an insert or that inserts are ineffective.

## Product and measured dose

Use a **ready-diluted, water-only BZK solution** with a clearly stated concentration. Do not substitute a fragranced cleaner, surgical scrub, or another product merely because it contains BZK. Do not buy 20%, 50%, or 80% concentrate to dilute at home.

One community contributor reports using **140 mL of BAK 1:750 approximately every six months**, with two years of use in their machine. This is **one user's experience, not a validated Pod dose or service interval**.[^community-regimen] sleepypod's existing guidance recommends liquid BZK but does not specify a tested quantity.[^water-care]

The reported product is Medical Chemical Corp **BAK 1:750**. Its printed Drug Facts label states **1.33 g/L BZK**, with water as the inactive ingredient. At that strength:

- **140 mL supplies about 186 mg of BZK.**
- **140 mL is about 4.7 US fl oz**, not exactly 5 fl oz.
- A product labeled exactly **0.13% w/v** supplies about **182 mg** in 140 mL.

Check the actual bottle label: the DailyMed page's structured strength field differs from its printed Drug Facts label. Resolve a conflicting concentration with the supplier before dosing. The product is labeled as a topical antiseptic; that label does not establish suitability or long-term seal compatibility inside a Pod.[^bzk-label]

**Do not add this full amount on every top-up, increase it to clear a clog, or layer it over an unknown existing treatment or active insert.** The reported six-month practice does not demonstrate that the previous dose is gone.

## Get treatment into the circulating water

Adding liquid to a full reserve tank does not establish that it reaches the cover. The community discussion disputes how much reserve water a routine prime exchanges.[^community-regimen]

When changing treatment, use the draining and refilling procedure supplied for your hardware. Account for liquid left in the cover and hub. Introduce the measured treatment during refill, when water is actually being drawn from the reservoir, and fill with distilled water to the marked line.[^water-refill] Do not mix BZK with peroxide, bleach, soap, or other cleaners; the BAK label specifically warns that soap and detergent destroy its germicidal properties.[^bzk-label]

Keep the bed empty during priming. A routine prime typically takes **2–5 minutes**; wait for it to finish before starting another.[^priming-operation] Daily priming is useful maintenance, but is not evidence that an entire reservoir dose has entered the loop.

### Reservoir size is not circuit volume

The manufacturer's setup guide says to have **about 2 L of distilled water** and expect **2–3 primes** for Pod 5 and earlier. It does not specify that every prime consumes a full 2 L reservoir, or establish the total circulating volume.[^water-refill] Multiplying these numbers into a claimed 4–6 L circuit would be unreliable.

For illustration only, 186 mg distributed evenly through 2 L, 4 L, or 6 L would be approximately **93, 47, or 31 mg/L (ppm)** respectively. These are dilution calculations, **not verified Pod capacities or recommended target concentrations**. A usable volume estimate requires measured water added during a documented refill, accounting for retained water and the reserve left outside the circulating loop.

## How long does BZK last?

**Do not assume BZK disappears after six months.** The BAK 1:750 safety data sheet describes it as stable under normal conditions; it gives no measured lifetime inside a heated Pod circuit.[^bzk-stability]

Distinguish three things:

- **Bottle shelf life:** follow your product's expiration date and storage instructions. One BZK manufacturer documents five years for its own products; that is not an expiry period for every dilute BZK bottle.[^shelf-life]
- **Chemical remaining in the system:** chemical stability does not mean the amount stays constant through draining, refilling, or contamination. Soap can also inactivate the treatment.[^bzk-label]
- **Effective protection:** none of these sources establishes how long a particular Pod dose suppresses growth. Bottle shelf life cannot establish a six-month maintenance interval.

There is no supported Pod-specific half-life here. Do not assume that an old dose has become harmless water, or repeatedly add a full dose on the assumption that nothing remains. Record the product, concentration, amount, and refill date.

## Do not use peroxide or other additives

**Do not use hydrogen peroxide in the sleepypod water-care regimen.** Peroxide can damage incompatible rubber seals and tubing. The effect depends on the elastomer and concentration: supplier compatibility tables give different ratings for different rubbers, so it would be inaccurate to claim that peroxide aggressively destroys every rubber material.[^seal-compatibility]

**Do not use bleach, humidifier/dehumidifier treatments, generic “anti-growth” additives, desiccants, or household cleaners.** Their full formulations have not been established as compatible with this circuit. Incompatible chemicals can damage seals; extra ingredients or particles introduce additional uncertainty. A BZK ingredient listing alone does not prove compatibility. Liquid BZK also lacks a verified lifetime compatibility study for every Pod generation.

Keep treatment in its labeled container, away from children and pets. Follow its handling instructions, avoid eye contact, and do not spray or mist it. If performance remains poor, check [water flow and thermal diagnostics](/troubleshooting/core/#powered-but-not-heating-or-cooling) instead of increasing the dose.
